# PRODUCT.md
## Acadza – AI-Powered Question Integrity

### 1. Who loses the most when a question is broken?

Students preparing for JEE and NEET lose the most. A single incorrect answer key, missing option or incomplete question can waste their preparation time, create conceptual confusion and damage their confidence. The impact multiplies when the same defective question is attempted by hundreds or thousands of students.

My design prioritises students by making error reporting simple, providing transparent updates and ensuring that reported issues are investigated rather than blindly accepted or dismissed. For faculty, reports are grouped by question and prioritised by potential impact, repetition and urgency, reducing repetitive manual investigation.

### 2. What would I never let AI auto-apply, and why?

I would never allow AI to independently rewrite an entire question, change mathematical conditions, alter scientific concepts, replace a solution without verification or modify fields outside the permitted repair taxonomy.

Even when AI identifies a likely defect, an incorrect correction can be more damaging than the original issue. Answer-key changes and ambiguous academic corrections should require independent verification and faculty approval.

Automatic application should only be permitted for explicitly authorised repair types after field-level validation and all required safety checks. Confidence alone is never sufficient justification for modifying academic content.

### 3. One metric I would watch in week one

**Verified Defect Resolution Rate within 24 hours.**

This measures the percentage of genuinely defective questions reported by students that are correctly identified, reviewed and resolved within 24 hours.

I would monitor false-positive classifications alongside this metric to ensure that faster resolution does not come at the cost of academic accuracy.

### 4. One idea beyond this brief

**Proactive Question Health Monitoring.**

I would introduce a Question Health Score for every question in Acadza's question bank, based on repeated reports, verified defects, correction history and automated content-consistency checks.

This would help identify potentially defective questions even before students report them, allowing faculty to investigate and correct issues proactively.

The long-term vision is to move Acadza from reacting to student-reported errors towards continuously maintaining the quality and reliability of its entire question bank.
